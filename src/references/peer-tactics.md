# Peer tactics

Optional toolbox for a brief, follow-up or correction with an open question about anchoring, premise, challenge or alternatives. Pick one or two tactics for the open question, risk and effort bound; no checklist, and clear tasks need none. A tactic creates no scope, write authority, seat or acceptance obligation. REOPEN_REQUEST, DEPENDENCY_REQUEST and BLOCKED are existing invariants, not options. Consensus and judgment counts are not proof.

Brief: separate authoritative intent/constraints, facts, assumptions and proposal. The Peer chooses method within scope and may reject the proposal. Follow-ups ask for evidence that could change the decision, not implementation steps.

- Progressive disclosure: Proposal may anchor judgment; give outcome, constraints, facts, unknowns first; preferred answer or prior findings after, within neutral/sealed rules. Hide nothing needed.
- Step-back: Many patches, unclear premise/contract; set the proposal aside; restate outcome, invariant, actual failure; no full re-analysis at every checkpoint.
- Reframing permission: Brief holds unproven assumptions; Peer may propose a framing fix with evidence and stop dependent work; scope change stays with its owner.
- Disconfirmation-first: Confident claim, weak proof; name the observation that would refute it and check that first; no forced findings when evidence suffices.
- Assumption inversion: One assumption drives the design; invert it, compare consequences and distinguishing evidence; settled requirements/authority stay fixed.
- Deletion test: Adding layer, gate or abstraction; ask which failure returns without it and which proof catches it; label inference versus verified. Mutants only in assigned scope, under R1/R2.
- Pre-mortem: Hard-to-reverse migration/integration; list a few material failures, mitigations, owners before execution; no exhaustive audit or extra small-task report.
- Reference class: Choosing topology, strategy or effort bound; compare with similar evidenced tasks, state differences; invent no rates.
- Contrastive decision: Several valid options meet the intent; compare on the same facts, trade-offs, reversal conditions; no strawman.
- Independent A/B: Costly lock-in justifies extra judgment; neutral briefs, sealed reports if needed, a separate question per seat; no forked answers, votes or default second seat.
- Steelman first: Judgments disagree; state the other side's strongest case before challenging with evidence; do not force consensus.
- Belief update: New evidence changes judgment; record prior claim, new evidence, changed judgment, reason in existing shared state; keep counterevidence, no false probabilities.
- Socratic follow-up: Reasoning or proof gap unclear; one open question that could change the decision; no leading.
- Unknown-unknown: New boundary has unsurveyed areas; ask which failure class or dependency lacks owner/proof, bounded by acceptance and budget; no repo-wide audit.
- Decision/reopen contract: Challenge needs a working route; Peer states failed premise, consequence, evidence, needed decision; Lead returns continuation, amendment or reasoned blocker.

Examples: uncertain premise, reframing permission; new abstraction, deletion test; repeated corrections, step-back.
